# Turbuhaler Inhaler Technique Evaluation Checklist

## Scope

This checklist applies to **AstraZeneca Turbuhaler** dry powder inhalers (DPI). Products include budesonide (Pulmicort Turbuhaler), formoterol (Oxis Turbuhaler), budesonide-formoterol (Symbicort Turbuhaler), and terbutaline (Bricanyl Turbuhaler).

## Critical Conceptual Difference From pMDI

Turbuhaler is breath-actuated and requires **fast, deep inhalation** to aerosolize the powder — this is the **opposite** of pMDI's slow inhalation requirement. This single difference is the source of the most common Turbuhaler errors when patients have switched from pMDI.

## Evaluation Framework

- **7 sequential steps** (T1–T7), each scored 0–3
- **2 conditional steps** (T8 ICS rinse, T9 storage) — same as pMDI
- **Maximum total score**: 21 (or 27 with conditional)
- Critical errors override score-based verdict to FAIL

## Step-by-Step Checklist

### T1 — Hold upright, unscrew and remove cover

**Action**: Hold the Turbuhaler upright (mouthpiece up) and unscrew the white cover. Inspect mouthpiece for foreign objects or moisture.

**Why it matters**: Upright orientation is required for the dose-loading mechanism to function. Tilting during the next step causes the powder to fall out of the metering chamber.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Holds upright, smoothly unscrews cover, brief mouthpiece inspection
- **Level 2** — Holds upright, unscrews cover, no inspection
- **Level 1** — Partially tilted while removing cover
- **Level 0** — Held inverted or sideways

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-TBH-01` "Holding upside-down during cover removal or loading"

**Observable in video**: Hand orientation, cover removal motion, mouthpiece visibility.

---

### T2 — Twist grip to load dose (must hear click)

**Action**: With device still upright, twist the colored grip at the base fully to one side and then back. A **click** must be heard, confirming dose has been loaded into the inhalation channel.

**Why it matters**: This is the dose metering step. Without a click, the dose has not been loaded — subsequent inhalation will deliver nothing. **The most diagnostic single criterion**: the click sound.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Full twist with audible click; device kept upright throughout
- **Level 2** — Click audible but device tilted briefly during twist
- **Level 1** — Twist motion observed but no audible click OR device significantly tilted
- **Level 0** — No twist OR loaded multiple times before inhaling (overdose)

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-TBH-02` "Loading without click" — no dose delivered
- `CRIT-TBH-03` "Multiple loading without inhalation" — overdose / wasted doses

**Observable in audio**: Click sound (high diagnostic value — even more reliable than visual)
**Observable in video**: Twist motion (left, then right), device orientation maintained.

---

### T3 — Exhale gently AWAY from mouthpiece

**Action**: Move the Turbuhaler away from the mouth, exhale gently to functional residual capacity. Do NOT exhale into the mouthpiece.

**Why it matters**: This is the **most clinically critical Turbuhaler-specific error**. Moisture from exhaled breath clumps the powder, blocking the dose. Even a single exhalation into the mouthpiece may render the next dose ineffective. This differs from pMDI where exhalation-into-mouthpiece causes dose loss; in Turbuhaler it also damages the device for subsequent doses.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Audible exhalation with device clearly held away from mouth
- **Level 2** — Exhalation observed, device held to the side (not perfectly away)
- **Level 1** — Minimal or no exhalation, but device not exhaled into
- **Level 0** — Exhalation INTO the mouthpiece (any duration)

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-TBH-04` "Exhalation into mouthpiece" — moisture damages powder

**Observable in video + audio**: Device position relative to mouth, exhalation sound direction.

---

### T4 — Place mouthpiece between teeth, lip seal

**Action**: Place mouthpiece between teeth, close lips firmly around it. Tongue should be flat at the bottom of the mouth.

**Why it matters**: Air leakage reduces the inspiratory flow required for powder aerosolization. Unlike pMDI, the DPI relies on the patient's own breath energy to disperse and aerosolize the powder — air leaks compromise this fundamentally.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Tight lip seal, mouthpiece between teeth, tongue out of way
- **Level 2** — Lip seal present, tongue position not verifiable
- **Level 1** — Visible gap or partial seal
- **Level 0** — Mouthpiece not properly in mouth

**Critical error mapping**: None directly listed (subsumed under T5 inhalation effectiveness)

**Observable in video**: Lip closure, cheek puffing (= air leak), mouthpiece insertion depth.

---

### T5 — Inhale FORCEFULLY and DEEPLY (most critical step)

**Action**: Inhale **fast, deep, and forceful** through the mouthpiece from the very start. Target inspiratory flow ≥60 L/min, sustained for the full inhalation. The first 0.5 seconds of inhalation are the most important for powder aerosolization.

**Why it matters**: This is **the** Turbuhaler-specific critical step. The powder is aerosolized by turbulence created by inspiratory airflow through the device's swirl chamber. If inspiratory flow is too slow (<30 L/min), the powder does not aerosolize properly and clumps fall into the mouth/throat. CRITIKAL identified slow inhalation as the most prevalent DPI error in their cohort.

**Common patient error**: Patients trained on pMDI ("breathe slow") apply the same slow technique to Turbuhaler and deliver almost no drug to the lungs.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Inhalation visibly fast and deep from the start; estimated PIF ≥60 L/min based on chest expansion speed and inhalation sound
- **Level 2** — Inhalation moderately fast (estimated 40-60 L/min) — sub-optimal but functional
- **Level 1** — Inhalation slow-to-moderate (~30-40 L/min) — major deviation, partial aerosolization expected
- **Level 0** — Inhalation slow (pMDI-style) — minimal aerosolization, dose effectively lost

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-TBH-05` "Inhalation too slow" — most prevalent critical DPI error per CRITIKAL
- `CRIT-TBH-06` "Inhalation not started forcefully" — initial flow critical

**Observable in audio**: Inhalation sound intensity and onset speed (highly diagnostic for PIF)
**Observable in video**: Speed of chest expansion, inspiratory duration.

---

### T6 — Continue deep inhalation to full lung capacity

**Action**: Continue inhaling until lungs are full (total lung capacity, approximately 3-5 seconds total). Effort throughout — not just the first burst.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Sustained forceful inhalation to apparent full inflation, ~3-5 sec
- **Level 2** — Inhalation continues 2-3 sec, partial chest expansion
- **Level 1** — Brief inhalation <2 sec, effort fades quickly
- **Level 0** — Inhalation aborted very early

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-TBH-07` "Inhalation truncated" — partial dose

**Observable in video + audio**: Duration of inhalation, chest expansion magnitude.

---

### T7 — Hold breath 10 seconds, remove mouthpiece, exhale slowly AWAY

**Action**: Remove mouthpiece. Hold breath for ~10 seconds. Exhale slowly through pursed lips, **away from the device** (still important to avoid moisture if re-using).

**Why it matters**: Breath-hold allows powder particles to deposit by gravitational and diffusional mechanisms onto airway walls. Same principle as pMDI.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Breath-hold ≥10 sec, slow controlled exhalation away from device
- **Level 2** — Breath-hold 5-9 sec
- **Level 1** — Breath-hold 1-4 sec
- **Level 0** — No breath-hold (immediate exhalation)

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-TBH-08` "No breath-hold"

**Observable in video**: Mouth closure, time to next exhalation, exhalation direction.

---

### T8 (conditional, ICS only) — Rinse mouth

**Action**: For ICS-containing Turbuhaler (Pulmicort, Symbicort) — rinse mouth with water, gargle, and spit out.

**Why it matters**: Same as pMDI ICS — prevents oral candidiasis and dysphonia. Particularly important for Turbuhaler because powder deposition in the oropharynx is typically higher than HFA pMDI.

**Rubric**: Same as pMDI S8.

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-TBH-09` "No mouth rinse with ICS" — long-term side effects.

---

### T9 (conditional) — Replace cover, store upright, dry

**Action**: Screw cover back on. Store the Turbuhaler upright in a dry place (not in a bathroom). Note the dose counter (red mark in window = ~20 doses remaining; empty = discard).

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cover replaced firmly, device set down upright
- **Level 2** — Cover replaced, device positioned non-upright
- **Level 0** — Cover not replaced (device exposed to moisture)

## Critical Error Summary — Turbuhaler

| ID | Description | Step | Clinical Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRIT-TBH-01 | Holding upside-down during cover removal/loading | T1, T2 | Powder falls out, no dose |
| CRIT-TBH-02 | Loading without click | T2 | No dose loaded |
| CRIT-TBH-03 | Multiple loading without inhalation | T2 | Overdose / wasted doses |
| CRIT-TBH-04 | Exhalation into mouthpiece | T3 | Moisture clumps powder, current AND next dose lost |
| CRIT-TBH-05 | Inhalation too slow (PIF <30 L/min) | T5 | Powder not aerosolized — most common DPI error per CRITIKAL |
| CRIT-TBH-06 | Inhalation not started forcefully from t=0 | T5 | First-burst aerosolization lost |
| CRIT-TBH-07 | Inhalation truncated | T6 | Partial dose |
| CRIT-TBH-08 | No breath-hold | T7 | Exhalation loss |
| CRIT-TBH-09 | No mouth rinse with ICS | T8 | Long-term oropharyngeal side effects |

**Per CRITIKAL DPI subgroup (n≈1,500)**: Slow inhalation was the dominant critical error (38% of DPI users), followed by no breath-hold (35%) and exhalation into mouthpiece (17%).

## Overall Verdict Logic

Same thresholds as pMDI:

```
IF any critical error detected:
    verdict = FAIL
ELSE IF total_score >= 18 (86%):
    verdict = PROFICIENT
ELSE IF total_score >= 14 (67%):
    verdict = ADEQUATE_WITH_EDUCATION
ELSE IF total_score >= 10 (48%):
    verdict = NEEDS_INTENSIVE_TRAINING
ELSE:
    verdict = FAIL
```

## VLM Observation Guidance

| Step | Visual reliability | Audio reliability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 (upright + cover) | High | Low | Hand orientation is key |
| T2 (twist + click) | High | **VERY HIGH** | Click sound is the single most diagnostic cue |
| T3 (exhale away) | High | High | Direction of exhalation visually evident |
| T4 (seal) | High | Low | Same as pMDI |
| T5 (FAST inhale) | Moderate | **VERY HIGH** | Inhalation sound intensity is most diagnostic |
| T6 (deep) | Moderate | High | Sustained sound vs. quick fade |
| T7 (hold) | High | High | Silence duration |
| T8 (rinse) | High | Moderate | Same as pMDI |

**Critical recommendation for VLM agents**: For Turbuhaler, **audio analysis is MORE diagnostic than video** at steps T2 (click) and T5 (inspiratory force). Multimodal analysis is essential. Audio-only evaluation may approach video-equivalent accuracy for this device.

## Switching Patient Notes (Clinical Context)

When evaluating a patient who has switched from pMDI to Turbuhaler:
- Pay extra attention to T5 (inhalation speed) — patients reliably under-perform here
- Expect coordination errors to disappear (good — no longer needed)
- Watch for residual habits like "head turn during inhalation" carried over from pMDI
