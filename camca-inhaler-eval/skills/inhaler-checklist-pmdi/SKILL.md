# pMDI Inhaler Technique Evaluation Checklist

## Scope

This checklist applies to **standalone pressurized metered-dose inhalers (pMDI)** without a spacer. Devices include albuterol/salbutamol HFA, fluticasone HFA, beclomethasone HFA, fluticasone-salmeterol HFA, budesonide-formoterol HFA, and similar.

For pMDI + spacer (e.g., AeroChamber, OptiChamber), use `inhaler-checklist-pmdi-spacer` (separate skill).

## Evaluation Framework

- **7 sequential steps** (S1–S7), each scored 0–3
- **2 conditional steps** (S8, S9) for ICS-containing inhalers
- **Maximum total score**: 21 (or 27 with conditional steps)
- **Critical errors**: separately tracked; a single critical error = overall FAIL regardless of other scores

## Step-by-Step Checklist

### S1 — Shake the inhaler

**Action**: Vigorously shake the inhaler 4-5 times to mix propellant and active drug.

**Why it matters**: HFA propellants in modern pMDIs separate from suspended active drug particles between actuations. Inadequate shaking results in inconsistent dose delivery (Berry RB et al. 2003).

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Shakes vigorously 4 or more times before first actuation
- **Level 2** — Shakes 2–3 times (acceptable for HFA in real-world per pragmatic interpretation; sub-optimal per strict)
- **Level 1** — Shakes only 1 time or with minimal vigor
- **Level 0** — Does not shake at all

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-pMDI-01` "No shaking" — clinically critical for solution-based pMDIs and most suspension formulations.

**Observable in video**: Hand motion (lateral shake), audible click of propellant mixing.

---

### S2 — Remove the cap and inspect

**Action**: Remove protective cap, visually inspect mouthpiece for foreign objects.

**Why it matters**: Forgotten caps and obstructed mouthpieces are common causes of dose loss. Several case reports document aspiration of forgotten cap fragments.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Removes cap, briefly inspects mouthpiece interior
- **Level 2** — Removes cap, no inspection
- **Level 1** — Removes cap with difficulty or only partially
- **Level 0** — Forgets to remove cap

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-pMDI-02` "Cap not removed" — total dose loss.

**Observable in video**: Cap removal motion, brief pause to look at mouthpiece.

---

### S3 — Exhale gently away from inhaler

**Action**: Breathe out fully to functional residual capacity (FRC), turning head away from the inhaler so as not to blow into it.

**Why it matters**: Exhaling into the mouthpiece dispels the metered dose. Failing to exhale before inhaling reduces lung volume available for the dose.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Audible/visible full exhalation, head turned away from mouthpiece
- **Level 2** — Partial exhalation OR exhalation without turning head away
- **Level 1** — Minimal exhalation
- **Level 0** — No exhalation OR exhales into the mouthpiece

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-pMDI-03` "Exhalation into the inhaler" — significant dose loss.

**Observable in video**: Chest deflation, audible exhalation sound, head orientation.

---

### S4 — Place mouthpiece in mouth with lip seal

**Action**: Place mouthpiece between teeth (or just inside the lips), close lips to form an airtight seal around the mouthpiece. Tongue should be flat, not blocking the mouthpiece.

**Why it matters**: Air leakage around the mouthpiece dilutes the aerosol with room air. Tongue obstruction deflects spray to the soft palate, increasing oral deposition.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Tight lip seal, mouthpiece between teeth, no visible tongue obstruction
- **Level 2** — Lip seal present but tongue position unclear OR mouthpiece outside teeth
- **Level 1** — Visible gap between lips and mouthpiece
- **Level 0** — Mouthpiece not in mouth OR major air leak

**Critical error mapping**: None directly (sub-optimal but rarely dose-invalidating). However, combined with poor inhalation, can compound.

**Observable in video**: Lip closure, mouthpiece visibility, cheek puffing (= air leak indicator).

---

### S5 — Begin slow inhalation AND press canister simultaneously

**Action**: Start a slow, deep inhalation (target inspiratory flow ~30 L/min, lasting ~3 seconds) and press the canister at the **start** of inhalation. The coordination of inhalation start and actuation is the single most error-prone step in pMDI technique.

**Why it matters**: This is the **single most important step** for pMDI dose delivery. Common errors:
- Pressing canister BEFORE inhaling → spray deposits in mouth/oropharynx
- Pressing canister AFTER inhalation complete → dose missed entirely
- Inhaling too rapidly (>60 L/min) → high oropharyngeal impaction, low lung deposition

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Inhalation starts smoothly, canister pressed within first 0.5 sec of inhalation; flow appears slow and controlled
- **Level 2** — Inhalation starts, canister pressed within first 1 sec; flow somewhat rushed but coordinated
- **Level 1** — Inhalation and actuation occur but timing off by >1 sec OR flow clearly too fast
- **Level 0** — Pressing canister before inhaling, OR pressing after inhalation complete, OR no actuation observed

**Critical error mapping**: 
- `CRIT-pMDI-04` "Actuation before inhalation" — dose lost to oropharynx
- `CRIT-pMDI-05` "Actuation after inhalation complete" — dose not inhaled
- `CRIT-pMDI-06` "Inhalation too fast" — minimal lung deposition

**Observable in video + audio**: Timing of canister press (visible motion of thumb/finger) vs. start of inhalation sound; inhalation duration; possibly audible aerosol release.

---

### S6 — Continue slow deep inhalation to full lung capacity

**Action**: Continue inhaling slowly and deeply through the mouthpiece until lungs are full (total lung capacity, ~3-5 seconds total inhalation time).

**Why it matters**: Truncated inhalation leaves dose in the oropharynx and central airways; deep inhalation maximizes peripheral airway deposition where the drug acts.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Sustained slow inhalation lasting ≥3 sec, visible chest expansion to apparent full inflation
- **Level 2** — Inhalation 2-3 sec, partial chest expansion
- **Level 1** — Brief inhalation <2 sec
- **Level 0** — Inhalation aborted or no observable inhalation

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-pMDI-07` "Inhalation truncated" — partial dose delivery.

**Observable in video + audio**: Duration of inhalation sound, chest expansion.

---

### S7 — Hold breath 10 seconds, then exhale slowly

**Action**: Remove the mouthpiece from the lips, close the mouth, hold breath for approximately 10 seconds (or as long as comfortable). Then exhale slowly through pursed lips or nose.

**Why it matters**: Breath-hold allows aerosol sedimentation onto airway walls (gravitational and diffusional deposition). Without breath-hold, a significant fraction of the dose is exhaled.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Breath-hold ≥10 sec, then slow exhalation
- **Level 2** — Breath-hold 5-9 sec
- **Level 1** — Breath-hold 1-4 sec
- **Level 0** — No breath-hold (immediate exhalation)

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-pMDI-08` "No breath-hold" — substantial dose loss.

**Observable in video**: Mouth closure, chest position, time to next exhalation.

---

### S8 (conditional, ICS only) — Rinse mouth and gargle

**Action**: For inhalers containing inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) — e.g., fluticasone, beclomethasone, budesonide — rinse the mouth with water and spit out (do not swallow).

**Why it matters**: Reduces oropharyngeal candidiasis (thrush) and dysphonia. Oral candidiasis affects 5-10% of regular ICS users without rinsing.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Rinses mouth and gargles, spits out water
- **Level 2** — Rinses mouth, no gargle
- **Level 1** — Drinks water without rinsing
- **Level 0** — No oral care

**Critical error mapping**: Not dose-affecting, but `CRIT-pMDI-09` "No mouth rinse with ICS" flagged for long-term side effect risk.

**Observable in video**: Glass of water, rinsing motion, spitting.

---

### S9 (conditional) — Replace cap and store properly

**Action**: Replace the protective cap. Store at room temperature, away from extreme heat.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cap replaced
- **Level 2** — Cap placed but loose
- **Level 0** — Cap not replaced

**Critical error mapping**: None for current dose; relevant for next dose.

## Critical Error Summary

A single critical error = **overall FAIL** regardless of total score.

| ID | Description | Step | Clinical Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRIT-pMDI-01 | No shaking (suspension formulations) | S1 | Inconsistent dose |
| CRIT-pMDI-02 | Cap not removed | S2 | Total dose loss |
| CRIT-pMDI-03 | Exhalation into inhaler | S3 | Significant dose loss |
| CRIT-pMDI-04 | Actuation before inhalation | S5 | Dose lost to oropharynx |
| CRIT-pMDI-05 | Actuation after inhalation complete | S5 | Dose missed |
| CRIT-pMDI-06 | Inhalation too fast | S5 | Minimal lung deposition |
| CRIT-pMDI-07 | Inhalation truncated | S6 | Partial dose |
| CRIT-pMDI-08 | No breath-hold | S7 | Substantial exhalation loss |
| CRIT-pMDI-09 | No mouth rinse (ICS) | S8 | Long-term oropharyngeal side effects (not acute dose) |

**Per CRITIKAL study (Price DB et al. 2017)**: The most common clinically critical pMDI errors in 3,660 patients were:
1. Inhalation coordination (S5): 45% of patients
2. Inadequate breath-hold (S7): 38% of patients
3. Exhalation into the mouthpiece (S3): 14% of patients

## Overall Verdict Logic

```
IF any critical error detected:
    verdict = FAIL
    reason = "Critical error(s) invalidate dose delivery"
ELSE IF total_score >= 18 (86%):
    verdict = PROFICIENT
ELSE IF total_score >= 14 (67%):
    verdict = ADEQUATE_WITH_EDUCATION
ELSE IF total_score >= 10 (48%):
    verdict = NEEDS_INTENSIVE_TRAINING
ELSE:
    verdict = FAIL
    reason = "Multiple severe deviations"
```

## VLM Observation Guidance

When the evaluator agent is a VLM analyzing video, the following channels are most reliable per step:

| Step | Visual reliability | Audio reliability |
|---|---|---|
| S1 (shake) | High (hand motion) | High (mixing sound) |
| S2 (cap) | High | Low |
| S3 (exhale) | Moderate (chest motion) | High (exhalation sound) |
| S4 (seal) | High (lip position) | Low |
| S5 (timing) | Moderate (finger motion) | High (inhalation onset + aerosol release timing) |
| S6 (deep) | Moderate (chest expansion) | High (inhalation duration) |
| S7 (hold) | High (chest position, no exhalation) | High (silence duration) |
| S8 (rinse) | High | Moderate |
| S9 (cap) | High | Low |

**Recommendation**: Combine visual + audio for S3, S5, S6, S7 — these benefit most from multimodal analysis.
