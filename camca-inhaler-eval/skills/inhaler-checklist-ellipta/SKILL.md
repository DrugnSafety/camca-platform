# Ellipta Inhaler Technique Evaluation Checklist

## Scope

This checklist applies to the **GSK Ellipta** multi-dose blister-strip dry powder inhaler. All Ellipta products share one identical device body, one identical dose-loading action, and therefore one checklist:

- **Relvar Ellipta / Breo Ellipta** — fluticasone furoate + vilanterol (ICS/LABA) — *ICS-containing*
- **Trelegy Ellipta** — fluticasone furoate + umeclidinium + vilanterol (ICS/LAMA/LABA) — *ICS-containing*
- **Anoro Ellipta** — umeclidinium + vilanterol (LAMA/LABA) — not ICS-containing
- **Incruse Ellipta** — umeclidinium (LAMA) — not ICS-containing
- **Arnuity Ellipta** — fluticasone furoate (ICS) — *ICS-containing*

Combination products containing two active strips (Relvar/Breo, Anoro, Trelegy) pierce **two blisters simultaneously** with a single cover slide; single-strip products (Incruse, Arnuity) pierce one. This is invisible to the patient and does not change technique or scoring — the cover is opened **once**, and one inhalation takes the whole dose.

**Use a sibling skill instead for other devices:**

| Device | Skill |
|---|---|
| Bare pMDI, no spacer | `inhaler-checklist-pmdi` |
| pMDI with spacer / valved holding chamber (mouthpiece or face-mask) | `inhaler-checklist-pmdi-spacer` |
| AstraZeneca Turbuhaler (twist-grip DPI) | `inhaler-checklist-turbuhaler` |
| GSK Diskus / Accuhaler, Wixela Inhub (thumbgrip + lever DPI) | `inhaler-checklist-diskus` |

Confirm the device from the `device-id` output before scoring. On video the Ellipta is distinguishable by its flat, wedge-shaped body with a **single sliding cover** on the front face, a rectangular dose-counter window on the top, and a colour-coded cover (e.g. light blue Relvar/Breo, red Anoro, green Incruse, grey/pale Trelegy). It has **no lever, no thumbgrip and no twist grip** — if a lever or a twist is visible, the device is a Diskus or a Turbuhaler and the wrong checklist has been selected.

## Critical Conceptual Differences

**Versus pMDI**: Ellipta is **breath-actuated**. There is no propellant, no shaking, and no hand-breath coordination requirement; the patient's own inspiratory flow must strip and aerosolize the powder from the pierced blister. Inhalation must be **long, steady, forceful and deep** — the opposite of the slow inhalation taught for pMDI. Patients switching from pMDI reliably under-perform on this step.

**Versus Turbuhaler and Diskus — the defining Ellipta simplification**: opening the cover **is** the dose-loading action. There is no separate lever to push and no grip to twist. Sliding the cover down until it clicks simultaneously exposes the mouthpiece, pierces the blister(s) and advances the dose counter. This makes the device fewer-step and lower-error in practice, but it introduces two Ellipta-specific failure modes that do not exist on the other DPIs:

1. **A partial cover slide loads nothing.** If the cover is not pushed all the way down until the click, the blister is not pierced. The patient sees an exposed mouthpiece and assumes a dose is ready when it is not.
2. **Closing the cover discards the loaded dose.** Once the cover has been slid down, the dose is committed. If the patient closes the cover and reopens it — a very common "let me start over" or "did I do that right?" behaviour — the first dose is **lost**, the counter decrements a second time, and the strip is exhausted early. There is no way to recover the wasted dose.

**Orientation**: hold the Ellipta **level/horizontal** with the mouthpiece toward the mouth and the dose counter facing up. Unlike Turbuhaler, an upright vertical hold is *not* required; do not penalise a horizontal hold. Once the cover is open, however, the powder sits loose in a pierced blister — **do not shake, tilt sharply, invert or drop** the device between opening and inhaling.

**Air vent**: the Ellipta has an **air vent slot on the body immediately beside/below the mouthpiece**. Airflow through this vent is required for the powder to be entrained and delivered. Covering it with the fingers or the palm while gripping the device is a documented, frequent and entirely device-specific Ellipta error, and it is visually detectable on video — a rare instance where video is more diagnostic than audio.

**Inspiratory flow requirement**: the Ellipta is a **low-resistance** DPI. Adequate dose emission requires roughly **≥30 L/min peak inspiratory flow (PIF)**, with fine-particle delivery approaching plateau around **60 L/min** and above; the low resistance means most adults, including many with moderate-to-severe airflow limitation, can achieve a usable flow. Nevertheless, patients in acute exacerbation, the very elderly, and the significantly frail may not. If inspiratory effort scores Level 0-1 repeatedly, flag for possible **device-mismatch** and recommend clinician review of device suitability rather than education alone.

## Evaluation Framework

- **7 sequential core steps** (E1–E7), each scored 0–3
- **2 conditional steps** (E8 ICS mouth rinse, E9 closing/storage), each scored 0–3
- **Maximum core score**: **21** (27 with both conditional steps)
- Verdict thresholds are computed on the **core 21-point** score; conditional steps are reported separately and can raise a critical-error flag (E8) but do not change the denominator
- Critical errors override the score-based verdict to FAIL

## Step-by-Step Checklist

### E1 — Check the dose counter and hold the Ellipta level, cover still closed

**Action**: Before opening, hold the inhaler flat/level in one hand with the mouthpiece pointing toward the patient and the **dose counter window facing up**. Read the counter: it shows the number of doses remaining; **"0" means the inhaler is empty** and the last five doses are displayed in red. Do **not** slide the cover open until the patient is ready to take the dose immediately.

**Why it matters**: The counter is the only objective state indicator on the device, and pre-reading it establishes the baseline against which E2 loading is confirmed. Opening the cover "to have a look", to practise, or well in advance of inhaling commits and then wastes a dose — the Ellipta has no dummy or test-fire mode.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Device held clearly level with counter visible; patient checks the counter and confirms doses remain; cover kept closed until ready to inhale
- **Level 2** — Held level, cover kept closed until ready, counter not checked
- **Level 1** — Marked tilt or awkward grip, OR cover opened noticeably early (several seconds of idle handling with the cover open)
- **Level 0** — Cover opened and the device then set down, fiddled with, or the session interrupted before inhaling (dose committed and lost), OR an empty ("0") inhaler used

**Critical error mapping**: none at this step in isolation; an early open that leads to a close-and-reopen is captured at E2/E3 as `CRIT-ELP-02`.

**Observable in video**: Hand plane relative to horizontal, cover position (closed = mouthpiece hidden), whether the counter window is brought into the patient's line of sight, legibility of the counter digits.

---

### E2 — Slide the cover DOWN fully until a click is heard — this loads the dose

**Action**: Slide the cover **downward**, away from the mouthpiece, in one movement until it stops and a **click** is heard/felt. The mouthpiece is now exposed and the blister(s) pierced. Confirm the **dose counter has decremented by exactly one**. This single action is the entire dose-loading procedure — there is **no lever to push and no grip to twist**.

**Why it matters**: This is the dose-metering step and the single most diagnostic mechanical criterion for this device. A partial slide exposes the mouthpiece without piercing the blister, so the patient inhales through an unloaded device and receives nothing while believing the dose was taken. Conversely, sliding the cover down repeatedly — or closing and reopening it — **discards** doses: each full open-click consumes one dose from the strip irreversibly and drops the counter accordingly, exhausting a 30-dose inhaler early and, for once-daily maintenance ICS/LABA and ICS/LAMA/LABA products, leaving the patient untreated at the end of the month.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cover slid down through its full travel in one motion with an audible/visible click; device kept level; patient checks that the counter decremented by one
- **Level 2** — Full slide with click, device level, counter not checked
- **Level 1** — Slide incomplete (cover stops short, no click) OR device markedly tilted during the slide OR the slide is hesitant/stuttered but ultimately reaches the click
- **Level 0** — Cover never opened (patient inhales through a closed or unloaded device), OR cover closed and reopened / slid open ≥2 times before inhaling (dose(s) wasted)

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-ELP-01` "Cover not slid fully open / no click — dose not loaded" — no drug available to inhale
- `CRIT-ELP-02` "Cover closed and reopened before inhaling" — loaded dose irretrievably wasted, counter double-decrement, strip exhausted early

**Observable in video**: Downward travel of the cover, mouthpiece becoming exposed, cover reaching its end stop, dose-counter digits before vs. after.
**Observable in audio**: The cover click — a firm, single, mechanically distinct sound; **very high** diagnostic value.

---

### E3 — Do NOT shake, tilt, invert or close the device after loading

**Action**: Between the click and the inhalation, keep the Ellipta level and still, and move it directly to the mouth. Do not shake it, do not turn it over, do not tap it, and above all **do not close the cover**.

**Why it matters**: After the cover click, the metered powder sits loose in an open, pierced blister in the inhalation channel. Shaking, inverting or sharply tilting spills part or all of it into the device body, where it cannot be inhaled. Closing the cover is worse than spilling: it terminates the dose entirely and the counter will decrement again on reopening (`CRIT-ELP-02`). Shaking is a classic pMDI carry-over habit — pMDIs *must* be shaken, Ellipta must never be — and is one of the most frequent errors in patients using both device types.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Device kept level and still from click to mouth; no shake, tilt, tap, or cover movement; transit is prompt
- **Level 2** — Minor, brief tilt during transit; no shake and no cover movement
- **Level 1** — Noticeable shaking, tapping, or sustained tilt/inversion after loading
- **Level 0** — Cover closed (or partially closed) after loading and before inhaling, OR device inverted/dropped with visible powder loss

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-ELP-03` "Device shaken, tapped, tilted or inverted after loading" — powder spilled from the pierced blister
- `CRIT-ELP-02` "Cover closed and reopened before inhaling" (also scored here when observed at this step)

**Observable in video**: Device plane tracked frame-by-frame from click to mouth; shake/tap motions are unmistakable; cover position must remain fully down.
**Observable in audio**: Low value, except that a second click betrays a close/reopen.

---

### E4 — Exhale fully AWAY from the device

**Action**: Holding the inhaler away from the mouth — or turning the head away from the mouthpiece — breathe out **fully and gently** to comfortable emptying (roughly functional residual capacity). Never exhale into or across the mouthpiece.

**Why it matters**: Two independent reasons. First, a full exhalation before inhaling is what makes a deep, high-flow inspiration possible; patients who inhale from mid-lung volume cannot generate the inspiratory volume the device needs. Second, and specific to DPIs, exhaled breath is warm and saturated with humidity: blown into the mouthpiece it clumps the freshly pierced powder, degrading the current dose and potentially the mouthpiece channel for subsequent doses. This is a device-damaging error, not merely a dose-losing one.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Full, unhurried exhalation with the device clearly held away from the mouth or the head clearly turned away
- **Level 2** — Adequate exhalation, device held to the side rather than fully away, no air passing through the mouthpiece
- **Level 1** — Minimal, shallow or absent exhalation, but nothing exhaled into the device
- **Level 0** — Any exhalation into or across the mouthpiece, of any duration

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-ELP-04` "Exhalation into the mouthpiece" — humidity clumps the powder; current dose degraded and device contaminated

**Observable in video**: Distance and angle between mouthpiece and mouth, head turn, chest/abdominal deflation.
**Observable in audio**: Exhalation timbre — breath passing through the device channel sounds distinctly different from free exhalation; direction and duration.

---

### E5 — Lips sealed firmly around the mouthpiece, fingers CLEAR of the air vent

**Action**: Put the mouthpiece between the lips (and teeth) in the shaped contour and **close the lips firmly around it**. Keep the tongue flat and low, out of the mouthpiece opening. Critically, hold the device so that the **fingers do not cover the air vent** on the body of the inhaler beside the mouthpiece.

**Why it matters**: The Ellipta relies entirely on the patient's inspiratory flow, and that flow must enter through the air vent and pass across the pierced blister. Fingers or a palm resting over the vent choke the airflow path, so even a vigorous inhalation entrains little powder — the patient does everything else correctly and still receives a fraction of the dose. Separately, an incomplete lip seal lets room air in around the mouthpiece, reducing the pressure drop across the device and the flow through it. Both errors produce the same silent under-dosing, and neither gives the patient any sensory feedback.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Firm, complete lip seal around the mouthpiece; tongue clear; grip clearly away from the air vent, vent visibly unobstructed
- **Level 2** — Good lip seal; grip acceptable but vent partially shadowed/not fully verifiable from the camera angle (report the limitation)
- **Level 1** — Visible gap or loose seal (cheek puffing, audible leak), OR fingers resting partly over the air vent
- **Level 0** — Mouthpiece not properly in the mouth (lips outside/around the body, teeth clamped shut over the opening), OR air vent clearly and fully occluded by the hand

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-ELP-05` "Air vent blocked by fingers/palm" — inspiratory airflow path obstructed, dose largely undelivered despite adequate effort

**Observable in video**: **High** — lip closure, cheek puffing, mouthpiece insertion depth, and, with a frontal or three-quarter angle, the exact position of the fingers relative to the vent slot. This is the one Ellipta step where video is the primary evidence.
**Observable in audio**: Low; a whistling leak may occasionally be audible.

---

### E6 — One long, steady, FORCEFUL and deep inhalation

**Action**: Take **one single** inhalation through the mouthpiece: long, steady, deep and forceful **from the very first instant**, continuing until the lungs are full (about 3–5 seconds in most adults). Do not inhale in a series of short sniffs, and do not stop and restart. The patient may not taste or feel the dose — that is normal and is **not** evidence that the dose was missed.

**Why it matters**: This is the step that actually delivers the drug and, together with E2, the highest-yield step in the whole checklist. Powder is de-agglomerated and aerosolized by the turbulent energy of the inspiratory flow itself; below roughly 30 L/min the powder is not adequately dispersed and large clumps deposit in the mouth and oropharynx instead of the lungs. Sanchis et al. and the CRITIKAL analysis both identify insufficient inspiratory effort as the dominant DPI error, and CRITIKAL associated it with worse asthma control and increased exacerbation risk. Because the Ellipta is low-resistance, the patient feels little resistance to pull against, which paradoxically encourages a gentle, unhurried inhalation — the evaluator should not read "smooth and comfortable" as "adequate".

**Common patient error**: patients trained on pMDI ("breathe in slowly and gently") apply the same slow technique to the Ellipta and deliver almost nothing to the lungs.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Single continuous inhalation, forceful from onset, sustained to apparent full inflation (~3–5 sec); sharp, loud, sustained inspiratory sound consistent with PIF ≥60 L/min
- **Level 2** — Single continuous inhalation, moderately forceful (~40–60 L/min equivalent), reaching near-full inflation — sub-optimal but functional
- **Level 1** — Weak or slow inhalation (~30–40 L/min equivalent), OR adequate force but truncated (<2 sec / clearly incomplete inflation), OR broken into two or more separate breaths through the device
- **Level 0** — Slow, gentle, pMDI-style inhalation or a shallow sniff; effort clearly inadequate to aerosolize powder; dose effectively lost

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-ELP-06` "Insufficient / weak inhalation (PIF below threshold, not forceful from onset)" — powder not aerosolized; most prevalent critical DPI error per CRITIKAL
- `CRIT-ELP-07` "Inhalation truncated or split into multiple breaths" — partial dose emitted, remainder left in the device

**Observable in audio**: **VERY HIGH** — onset sharpness, intensity and duration of the inspiratory sound are the most informative available signals for inspiratory force.
**Observable in video**: **Low–Moderate** — inspiratory force is poorly assessable visually; speed and magnitude of chest/shoulder expansion is a weak proxy confounded by body habitus, clothing, camera angle and framing. **If audio is unavailable or unusable, score E6 with explicitly low confidence and state that PIF adequacy could not be assessed** rather than inferring a number from the video.

---

### E7 — Hold breath ~10 seconds, remove the inhaler, exhale slowly away

**Action**: Remove the inhaler from the mouth and hold the breath for **about 10 seconds**, or as long as is comfortable. Then breathe out **slowly and gently, away from the device**.

**Why it matters**: The breath-hold gives the fine particles suspended in the airways time to deposit by sedimentation and diffusion onto the airway walls. Without it, a substantial fraction of the still-airborne dose is simply exhaled. CRITIKAL found absent breath-hold to be among the most frequent errors across all device classes. Exhaling away from the device on completion protects the mouthpiece from humidity for the next dose.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Inhaler removed from the mouth and breath held ≥10 sec; exhalation slow, controlled and directed away from the device
- **Level 2** — Breath held 5–9 sec; exhalation away from device
- **Level 1** — Breath held 1–4 sec
- **Level 0** — No breath-hold (immediate exhalation), and/or exhalation directed into the mouthpiece

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-ELP-08` "No breath-hold" — airborne dose exhaled before deposition

**Observable in video**: Timing of device removal, closed mouth/held posture, time elapsed to first exhalation, exhalation direction.
**Observable in audio**: High — the silent interval between the end of the inspiratory sound and the onset of the expiratory sound is a reliable timer.

---

### E8 (conditional — ICS-containing products only) — Rinse mouth, gargle and spit

**Applies to**: Relvar/Breo Ellipta, Trelegy Ellipta, Arnuity Ellipta (all contain fluticasone furoate). Does **not** apply to Anoro Ellipta or Incruse Ellipta. Confirm the product before scoring this step; if the product is not ICS-containing, mark E8 *not applicable* rather than scoring zero.

**Action**: After the breath-hold and exhalation, rinse the mouth with water, gargle, and **spit the water out** — do not swallow it.

**Why it matters**: Oropharyngeal deposition of dry powder is substantial even with good technique, and retained corticosteroid predisposes to oral candidiasis, dysphonia/hoarseness and pharyngeal irritation. Spitting rather than swallowing also limits the swallowed fraction available for gastrointestinal absorption. For once-daily Ellipta ICS products this is a once-daily habit and is easy to establish.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Rinses, gargles and spits out
- **Level 2** — Rinses and spits without gargling
- **Level 1** — Rinses but swallows the water, or only drinks water
- **Level 0** — No rinse at all with an ICS-containing product

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-ELP-09` "No mouth rinse with ICS-containing product" — long-term local corticosteroid side effects (candidiasis, dysphonia)

**Observable in video**: Cup, bottle or sink in frame; head-back gargle posture; spitting action.
**Observable in audio**: Moderate — gargling is acoustically distinctive when captured.

---

### E9 (conditional) — Slide the cover UP to close; check the counter; store dry

**Action**: Slide the cover back **up** as far as it will go to cover the mouthpiece. Note the dose counter reading. Store the inhaler in a dry place, below the labelled temperature, away from bathrooms and other humid environments; keep it in its sealed foil tray with the desiccant until first use, and discard it after the labelled in-use period or when the counter reaches 0, whichever comes first.

**Why it matters**: Closing protects the mouthpiece and the internal channel from dust and ambient humidity — the same moisture sensitivity that makes exhaling into the device harmful applies to storage. Note that closing the cover **after** an inhalation is correct and does not waste a dose; it is closing *between* opening and inhaling that discards the dose (`CRIT-ELP-02`). Evaluators must not confuse the two.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cover slid fully up and closed; counter checked; device set down/stored in a dry, appropriate place
- **Level 2** — Cover closed fully; counter not checked or storage location not observable
- **Level 1** — Cover only partially closed (mouthpiece still exposed)
- **Level 0** — Cover left open, device left exposed or stored somewhere visibly humid (e.g. bathroom shelf, sink edge)

**Critical error mapping**: none (hygiene/durability step; report as an education point).

## Critical Error Summary — Ellipta

| ID | Description | Step | Clinical Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRIT-ELP-01 | Cover not slid fully down / no click — dose not loaded | E2 | Blister not pierced; no drug available; patient believes dose was taken |
| CRIT-ELP-02 | Cover closed and reopened before inhaling | E2, E3 | Loaded dose irretrievably wasted; counter double-decrement; inhaler exhausted early |
| CRIT-ELP-03 | Device shaken, tapped, tilted or inverted after loading | E3 | Powder spills from the open blister into the device body; partial or total dose loss |
| CRIT-ELP-04 | Exhalation into the mouthpiece | E4 | Humidity clumps the powder; current dose degraded and mouthpiece channel contaminated for later doses |
| CRIT-ELP-05 | Air vent blocked by fingers/palm | E5 | Inspiratory airflow path obstructed; dose largely undelivered despite adequate effort — Ellipta-specific error |
| CRIT-ELP-06 | Insufficient/weak inhalation (PIF below threshold; not forceful from onset) | E6 | Powder not aerosolized; drug deposits in the mouth rather than the lungs — most prevalent critical DPI error per CRITIKAL |
| CRIT-ELP-07 | Inhalation truncated or split into multiple breaths | E6 | Partial dose emitted; remainder retained in the device |
| CRIT-ELP-08 | No breath-hold | E7 | Suspended fine particles exhaled before airway deposition |
| CRIT-ELP-09 | No mouth rinse with ICS-containing product | E8 | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia, pharyngeal irritation over time |

**Interpretation note**: CRITIKAL (Price DB et al. 2017) identified insufficient inspiratory effort and absent breath-hold as the DPI errors most strongly and consistently associated with poorer asthma control and increased exacerbation rate; these map to `CRIT-ELP-06` and `CRIT-ELP-08`. `CRIT-ELP-01`, `CRIT-ELP-02` and `CRIT-ELP-05` are device-specific loading/airflow failures that are not separately enumerated in CRITIKAL but are functionally equivalent to "dose not delivered" and are therefore treated as critical here.

## Overall Verdict Logic

Same thresholds as the sibling checklists; computed on the **core 21-point** score (E1–E7):

```
IF any critical error detected:
    verdict = FAIL
ELSE IF total_score >= 18 (86%):
    verdict = PROFICIENT
ELSE IF total_score >= 14 (67%):
    verdict = ADEQUATE_WITH_EDUCATION
ELSE IF total_score >= 10 (48%):
    verdict = NEEDS_INTENSIVE_TRAINING
ELSE:
    verdict = FAIL
```

**Recommendations for VLM agents evaluating an Ellipta:**
1. **Count the clicks.** Exactly **one** cover click should occur before the inhalation, and (optionally) one after. Two clicks before the inhalation means the cover was closed and reopened — `CRIT-ELP-02`, a wasted dose. Clicks are the highest-value auditory feature after the inspiratory sound.
2. **Read the dose counter at the start and end of the clip.** It is the only objective, machine-readable state indicator on the device, and it is the only way to distinguish "loaded correctly" from "cover opened but blister not pierced" when the click is not audible.
3. **Look at the hand, not just the face, at E5.** The air vent is an Ellipta-specific failure point that no other checklist in this plugin contains, and it is one of the few technique errors better detected by video than by audio.
4. **Never silently guess at E6.** Report an explicit confidence level for the inspiratory-force judgement and downgrade it when audio is absent, the microphone is distant, or ambient noise masks the breath. An unassessable step must be reported as unassessed.
5. **Do not import Turbuhaler or Diskus mechanics.** There is no twist and no lever on an Ellipta; a patient searching for one has been mis-trained, which is itself a reportable finding. A horizontal hold is correct.

## Switching-Patient Notes (Clinical Context)

**From pMDI to Ellipta** — expect and specifically probe for:
- Slow, gentle inhalation (E6) — the most common and most damaging carry-over; the patient is applying correct pMDI technique to the wrong device
- Shaking the device before use (E3) — mandatory for pMDI, harmful once an Ellipta dose is loaded
- Waiting for a taste, spray sensation or "puff" feedback, and re-opening the cover to "try again" when none arrives (E2, `CRIT-ELP-02`) — Ellipta delivery is essentially imperceptible; counsel explicitly that the absence of sensation does not mean the absence of a dose, and that reopening the cover throws a dose away

**From Turbuhaler or Diskus to Ellipta** — expect:
- Hunting for a grip to twist or a lever to push, then treating the cover slide as a separate "opening" action and looking for the loading step afterwards (E2)
- For Turbuhaler switchers, an unnecessary upright vertical hold (E1, E2) — neutral, do not penalise, but correct it since the tilted transit that often follows is a real risk
- Generally *better* E6 performance, since forceful inhalation was already required by both prior devices

**Once-daily dosing context**: most Ellipta products (Relvar/Breo, Anoro, Incruse, Trelegy, Arnuity) are once-daily maintenance therapy. A single wasted dose therefore represents a whole day of missed maintenance treatment, and a habitual close-and-reopen behaviour will exhaust a 30-dose inhaler days early and produce an unexplained gap in therapy before the next refill. Treat `CRIT-ELP-02` as an adherence issue as well as a technique issue, and check refill intervals when it is observed.

**Patients using an Ellipta plus a pMDI reliever** (very common in COPD and in asthma maintenance) are at elevated risk of applying the wrong inhalation pattern to each. Evaluate and educate on both devices in the same session and make the "slow and steady for the pMDI, long and hard for the Ellipta" contrast explicit — this contrast is the single highest-yield teaching point for these patients.
