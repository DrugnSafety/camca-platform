# Respimat Soft Mist Inhaler (SMI) Technique Evaluation Checklist

## Scope

This checklist applies to the **Boehringer Ingelheim Respimat Soft Mist Inhaler (SMI)**, in both the original cartridge-insertion (reusable) and pre-assembled/disposable presentations. Covered products:

- **Spiriva Respimat** (tiotropium)
- **Spiolto Respimat / Stiolto Respimat** (tiotropium-olodaterol)
- **Striverdi Respimat** (olodaterol)
- **Combivent Respimat** (ipratropium-albuterol)

### Routing to sibling checklists

If the device in the recording is **not** a Respimat, do not use this skill. Route as follows:

| Device observed | Skill to use |
|---|---|
| Pressurized metered-dose inhaler, standalone | `inhaler-checklist-pmdi` |
| pMDI with valved holding chamber / spacer / face mask | `inhaler-checklist-pmdi-spacer` |
| AstraZeneca Turbuhaler (twist-grip DPI) | `inhaler-checklist-turbuhaler` |
| GSK Diskus / Accuhaler (lever, blister strip DPI) | `inhaler-checklist-diskus` |
| GSK Ellipta (hinged cover DPI) | `inhaler-checklist-ellipta` |
| AstraZeneca Genuair / Pressair (button DPI, colour window) | `inhaler-checklist-genuair` |

### Respimat is neither a pMDI nor a DPI — and why that changes scoring

- **Not a pMDI**: there is no HFA propellant and no pressurized canister. The dose is expelled by a mechanical spring forcing a metered volume of drug **solution** through a fine nozzle (the "uniblock"). The resulting aerosol cloud is emitted slowly over roughly **1.5 seconds** — an order of magnitude longer than a pMDI spray plume (~0.1–0.2 s). Consequently the *coordination* tolerance is much wider, and pMDI-style critical errors of actuation timing must not be scored with pMDI-level severity.
- **Not a DPI**: the drug is already aerosolized by the device, not by the patient's inspiratory effort. There is no minimum inspiratory flow requirement and **no benefit to forceful inhalation** — forceful inhalation is actively harmful because it increases oropharyngeal impaction and reduces lung deposition. DPI scoring logic (reward fast inhalation) must be **inverted** for Respimat.
- **Solution, not suspension**: **no shaking step exists.** Absence of shaking is *not* an error and must never be scored as one. Scoring a missing shake here would be a false-positive carried over from the pMDI checklist.

## Critical Conceptual Differences

Read this section before scoring. It governs the interpretation of R5 and R6.

1. **Inhalation must be SLOW and DEEP — not forceful.**
   The mist is already generated by the device. The patient's job is only to carry the mist into the lower airways with a gentle, sustained inspiration (target roughly ~30 L/min, similar to pMDI). Rapid or forceful inhalation increases inertial impaction in the oropharynx, wasting the dose in the throat. **This is the exact inverse of the DPI requirement** and is one of the most common cross-device confusions.

2. **The ~1.5-second mist duration makes coordination markedly EASIER than pMDI.**
   Because the aerosol is emitted slowly and over a long window, a patient who presses the dose-release button slightly before or slightly into the inhalation still captures most of the dose. Coordination is therefore *more forgiving*, but not optional: pressing the button before the mouth is on the mouthpiece, or after inhalation has ended, still loses the dose. Score coordination errors on R5 with this tolerance in mind.

3. **No shaking is required.** Respimat contains a solution. Shaking is unnecessary and its absence is not an error.

4. **Air vents are a Respimat-specific hazard.** The mouthpiece has air-intake vents on its sides. If the lips or fingers occlude these vents, entrained airflow is disrupted and delivery is compromised. This error has no analogue in pMDI or DPI checklists.

5. **First use requires priming.** A newly assembled device (cartridge inserted) must be actuated toward the ground until a **visible mist** appears (and per product information, primed further as instructed) before the first therapeutic dose. An unprimed first use delivers no reliable dose.

## Evaluation Framework

- **7 sequential core steps** (R1–R7), each scored 0–3
- **Maximum core score**: 21
- **2 conditional steps** (R8 ICS rinse — usually N/A; R9 cap closure/storage)
- Critical errors are tracked separately; a single critical error = overall **FAIL** regardless of total score

## Step-by-Step Checklist

### R1 — (First use only) Insert cartridge and prime until mist is visible; otherwise verify device readiness

**Action**: For a **new** device: with the clear base removed, push the cartridge into the inhaler until it clicks, replace the base, then hold the inhaler upright with the cap **closed**, turn the base half a turn until it clicks, open the cap, and actuate the dose-release button **pointing toward the ground** — repeating until a **visible mist** is produced, then priming further as the product information instructs. For an in-use device: verify the device is assembled and the dose indicator is not in the empty position.

**Why it matters**: An unprimed Respimat expels air or an incompletely filled metering volume — the first "doses" are not therapeutic. Patients who assemble a new device and immediately dose receive no reliable drug.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — New device: cartridge inserted to click, base replaced, primed toward the ground until visible mist and completed per instructions. In-use device: assembly and dose indicator explicitly checked.
- **Level 2** — Primed but fewer actuations than instructed, or mist visibility not confirmed; in-use device handled correctly but not checked.
- **Level 1** — Cartridge inserted but priming attempted only once or aimed at the face/upward.
- **Level 0** — New device used without any priming, or cartridge not fully seated.

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-RSM-07` "Device never primed at first use" — no reliable dose from a new device.

**Observable in video**: Cartridge insertion motion, base removal/replacement, device pointed at floor with repeated button presses, visible mist plume.

---

### R2 — Hold upright with cap CLOSED and turn the clear base half a turn until it clicks (load the dose)

**Action**: Hold the inhaler **upright** with the cap **closed**. Turn the **clear base** half a turn in the direction of the **arrows on the label** until it **clicks**. This compresses the spring and loads the metered dose.

**Why it matters**: This is the dose-metering step and the single highest-yield criterion on the device. Without the half-turn there is no loaded dose and the subsequent button press delivers nothing. Turning with the cap **open** can discharge the dose prematurely into the air. Turning less than a full half-turn does not engage the mechanism (no click).

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Device held upright, cap closed, full half-turn in the arrow direction with an audible/observable click
- **Level 2** — Half-turn completed with click, but device tilted or the turn was hesitant/two-stage
- **Level 1** — Turn attempted but incomplete / no click evident, or turned with the cap already open
- **Level 0** — Base never turned (no dose loaded), or base turned repeatedly without inhaling between turns (wasted doses)

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-RSM-01` "Base not turned / dose not loaded" — no dose delivered
- `CRIT-RSM-08` "Repeated base turns without inhalation" — doses wasted, dose indicator advances falsely

**Observable in video**: Rotation of the transparent base relative to the body; label arrows.
**Observable in audio**: The **click** is a key audio signal — high diagnostic value, comparable to the Turbuhaler twist click.

---

### R3 — Open the cap fully

**Action**: Flip the cap open until it **snaps fully back**, exposing the mouthpiece and the side air vents.

**Why it matters**: A partially opened cap obstructs the mouthpiece and can deflect the mist. It also prevents a proper lip seal and may block the air vents.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cap opened fully until it snaps back; mouthpiece unobstructed
- **Level 2** — Cap opened but not fully snapped back
- **Level 1** — Cap partially open, partially obstructing the mouthpiece
- **Level 0** — Cap not opened (dose released into a closed cap or not released at all)

**Critical error mapping**: covered under `CRIT-RSM-01` when the dose cannot exit; otherwise a graded deviation.

**Observable in video**: Cap hinge position, mouthpiece and vent visibility.

---

### R4 — Exhale fully and slowly AWAY from the device

**Action**: Turn the head away from the mouthpiece and breathe out fully and slowly to functional residual capacity, then bring the inhaler to the mouth.

**Why it matters**: Exhaling into the mouthpiece disperses the loaded mist path and wets the mouthpiece. Failing to exhale first leaves insufficient inspiratory reserve for the sustained slow breath needed to carry the mist to the lower airways.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Full, slow, audible exhalation with the head clearly turned away from the device
- **Level 2** — Adequate exhalation but head/device not clearly separated, or exhalation somewhat forced/rapid
- **Level 1** — Minimal or partial exhalation, but not into the mouthpiece
- **Level 0** — No exhalation, or exhalation directed into the mouthpiece

**Critical error mapping**: not a standalone critical error for SMI (no powder to spoil), but Level 0 with exhalation into the mouthpiece should be reported as a major deviation and contributes to dose loss.

**Observable in video + audio**: Head orientation relative to the device, chest deflation, exhalation sound.

---

### R5 — Seal lips around the mouthpiece WITHOUT covering the air vents

**Action**: Place the mouthpiece in the mouth, closing the lips firmly around it, with the inhaler pointing toward the back of the throat. **Do not let the lips or fingers cover the air vents** on the sides of the mouthpiece. Keep the tongue flat and out of the aerosol path.

**Why it matters**: This is a **Respimat-specific error with no pMDI or DPI analogue**. The vents admit the entrainment air that carries and dilutes the mist; occluding them with the lips (mouthpiece inserted too deeply) or with a gripping finger disturbs the plume and reduces delivered dose. Air leakage around the lips separately dilutes the mist with room air.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Firm lip seal, vents clearly unobstructed by lips and fingers, device aimed at the back of the throat, tongue flat
- **Level 2** — Lip seal adequate, vent status or tongue position not fully verifiable
- **Level 1** — Visible partial vent occlusion (finger resting near a vent) or a visible lip gap
- **Level 0** — Vents clearly covered by lips or fingers, or mouthpiece not properly in the mouth

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-RSM-02` "Air vents covered by lips or fingers" — disrupted mist delivery.

**Observable in video**: Grip position of the fingers relative to the vents, depth of mouthpiece insertion, lip closure, cheek puffing (air leak indicator).

---

### R6 — Press the dose-release button while inhaling SLOWLY and DEEPLY, and keep inhaling for the whole mist

**Action**: Begin a **slow, deep** inhalation and, at the start of that breath, press the **dose-release button**. Continue inhaling slowly for as long as comfortable — importantly, **through the entire ~1.5 second mist emission and beyond**, to total lung capacity (roughly 3–5 seconds of inhalation).

**Why it matters**: Two distinct failure modes live here.
- **Speed**: forceful/fast inhalation drives the mist into the oropharynx by inertial impaction. Because the aerosol is pre-generated, extra effort adds nothing and costs lung deposition. **This is the inverse of the DPI rule** and is the classic carryover error in patients switching from a DPI.
- **Duration/coordination**: the mist takes ~1.5 s to be delivered. A patient who stops inhaling after a short pMDI-style sniff cuts off the second half of the emission. Conversely, pressing the button before the mouthpiece is sealed in the mouth, or after inhalation has finished, loses the dose outright — although the long emission window makes small timing mismatches tolerable.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Slow, controlled inhalation; button pressed at/just after the start of the breath; inhalation continues smoothly well beyond the mist emission (≥3 s total), to apparent full inflation
- **Level 2** — Coordination adequate (button within the inhalation window) but inhalation slightly rushed or ~2–3 s, covering the mist but not to full inflation
- **Level 1** — Inhalation clearly too fast/forceful, OR inhalation ends at ~1–2 s so that the later part of the mist is not captured, OR button pressed >1 s before inhalation starts (mouthpiece sealed)
- **Level 0** — Button pressed before the mouthpiece is in the mouth, or after inhalation is complete, or no actuation observed, or violently forceful DPI-style inhalation

**Critical error mapping**:
- `CRIT-RSM-03` "Inhalation too fast or forceful" — oropharyngeal impaction; inverse of the DPI error and a common cross-device confusion
- `CRIT-RSM-04` "Actuation not coordinated with inhalation" — dose released outside the breath
- `CRIT-RSM-05` "Inhalation truncated before the mist finished" — later fraction of the ~1.5 s emission not captured

**Observable in video**: Thumb/finger press on the dose-release button, speed and magnitude of chest expansion, and — unusually for an inhaler — **the soft mist plume itself may be visible on video**, which is a rare and highly useful direct observation of emission timing and duration.
**Observable in audio**: Inhalation onset and duration; a *quiet* inspiratory sound is the desired finding here (loud, turbulent inspiration suggests excessive flow).

---

### R7 — Hold breath about 10 seconds, then exhale slowly

**Action**: Remove the mouthpiece from the lips, keep the mouth closed and hold the breath for approximately **10 seconds**, or as long as comfortable. Then exhale slowly through pursed lips or the nose, away from the device.

**Why it matters**: The breath-hold lets the fine droplets deposit onto airway walls by sedimentation and diffusion. Without it a substantial fraction of the still-suspended mist is exhaled. If a second dose is prescribed, repeat R2–R7 in full.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Breath-hold ≥10 s, followed by slow controlled exhalation away from the device
- **Level 2** — Breath-hold 5–9 s
- **Level 1** — Breath-hold 1–4 s
- **Level 0** — No breath-hold (immediate exhalation)

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-RSM-06` "No breath-hold" — substantial exhalation loss.

**Observable in video**: Mouth closure, static chest position, time elapsed before exhalation.
**Observable in audio**: Duration of silence between end of inspiration and onset of expiration.

---

### R8 (conditional — usually N/A for Respimat) — Rinse mouth if the product contains an ICS

**Action**: If, and only if, the inhaled product contains an inhaled corticosteroid, rinse the mouth with water, gargle, and spit out.

**Important — handle explicitly**: The marketed Respimat products (Spiriva, Spiolto/Stiolto, Striverdi, Combivent) are **LAMA and/or LABA (and SAMA/SABA) combinations and contain NO inhaled corticosteroid**. For these products this step is **N/A** and must be **excluded from the denominator** — do not score it 0 and do not raise a rinse-related critical error. Record it as `N/A (non-ICS product)`.

An anticholinergic-specific counselling point may be substituted in feedback: avoid spraying toward the eyes (risk of blurred vision / pupillary effects with ipratropium and tiotropium), and dry mouth is an expected minor effect.

**Rubric (applies only if an ICS-containing product is in fact being used)**:
- **Level 3** — Rinses and gargles, spits out water
- **Level 2** — Rinses mouth, no gargle
- **Level 1** — Drinks water without rinsing
- **Level 0** — No oral care
- **N/A** — Non-ICS product (the expected case for Respimat)

**Critical error mapping**: `CRIT-RSM-09` "No mouth rinse with ICS" — applicable only when an ICS product is used; not scored for standard Respimat products.

---

### R9 (conditional) — Close the cap, check the dose indicator, store properly

**Action**: Close the cap. Check the **dose indicator scale** on the side of the device: as doses are used the pointer moves toward the **red zone**, which signals the device is nearly empty and a replacement is needed; when the pointer reaches the end of the scale the device locks and must be discarded. Store at room temperature, protected from freezing.

**Rubric**:
- **Level 3** — Cap closed firmly and the dose indicator checked/verbalized
- **Level 2** — Cap closed, indicator not checked
- **Level 1** — Cap closed loosely or only partially
- **Level 0** — Cap left open

**Critical error mapping**: none for the current dose; relevant to adherence and to running out of medication unnoticed.

**Observable in video**: Cap closure; the **dose indicator is visually readable** on close-up frames — a rare opportunity to confirm remaining doses directly from the recording.

## Critical Error Summary — Respimat SMI

A single critical error = **overall FAIL** regardless of total score.

| ID | Description | Step | Clinical Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRIT-RSM-01 | Base not turned / dose not loaded | R2 (R3) | No dose delivered at all |
| CRIT-RSM-02 | Air vents covered by lips or fingers | R5 | Entrainment airflow disrupted, delivered dose reduced (Respimat-specific) |
| CRIT-RSM-03 | Inhalation too FAST or forceful | R6 | Oropharyngeal impaction, minimal lung deposition — **inverse of the DPI error**, common carryover in device switchers |
| CRIT-RSM-04 | Actuation not coordinated with inhalation (button pressed before mouthpiece sealed, or after inhalation ended) | R6 | Mist released outside the breath, dose lost |
| CRIT-RSM-05 | Inhalation truncated before the ~1.5 s mist finished | R6 | Later fraction of the emission not inhaled |
| CRIT-RSM-06 | No breath-hold | R7 | Suspended droplets exhaled before deposition |
| CRIT-RSM-07 | Device never primed at first use | R1 | New device delivers no reliable dose |
| CRIT-RSM-08 | Repeated base turns without inhalation between doses | R2 | Doses wasted; dose indicator advances without therapy |
| CRIT-RSM-09 | No mouth rinse when the product contains an ICS (not applicable to standard Respimat LAMA/LABA products) | R8 | Long-term oropharyngeal side effects (not an acute dose error) |

**Contextual note**: CRITIKAL (Price DB et al. 2017) characterized critical errors principally for pMDI and DPI cohorts; SMI-specific critical-error frequencies were not separately powered in that study. The IDs above apply the CRITIKAL *definition* of a critical error (an error expected to substantially reduce or nullify drug delivery to the lungs) to Respimat-specific mechanics. Do not attribute SMI-specific prevalence figures to CRITIKAL.

## Overall Verdict Logic

Same thresholds and form as the pMDI and Turbuhaler checklists. Denominator is the core 21 points; conditional steps scored `N/A` (the usual case for R8) are excluded from both numerator and denominator.

```
IF any critical error detected:
    verdict = FAIL
    reason = "Critical error(s) invalidate dose delivery"
ELSE IF total_score >= 18 (86%):
    verdict = PROFICIENT
ELSE IF total_score >= 14 (67%):
    verdict = ADEQUATE_WITH_EDUCATION
ELSE IF total_score >= 10 (48%):
    verdict = NEEDS_INTENSIVE_TRAINING
ELSE:
    verdict = FAIL
    reason = "Multiple severe deviations"
```

**Recommendation for VLM agents**: For Respimat, prioritize (a) the R2 click in audio, (b) the R5 finger/lip position on the vents in video, and (c) the visible mist plume at R6 when frame quality permits — the plume lets you verify actuation-to-inhalation alignment and whether inhalation continued through the full emission, something not directly observable in pMDI or DPI recordings. Note that inspiratory *loudness* is scored in the opposite direction from Turbuhaler: loud is bad here.

## Switching-Patient Notes (Clinical Context)

Device switching is the dominant source of Respimat technique error. Evaluate with the patient's prior device in mind.

- **Switching from a DPI (Turbuhaler, Diskus, Ellipta, Genuair) → Respimat**: expect **forceful over-inhalation** as a carryover error. These patients were explicitly trained to inhale "hard and fast"; applied to Respimat this drives the mist into the throat. Scrutinize R6 and expect `CRIT-RSM-03`. Also watch for a residual "prepare then sniff sharply" rhythm and for patients waiting for a powder-style resistance that never comes.
- **Switching from a pMDI → Respimat**: expect **inhalation stopped too early**. pMDI users are conditioned to a near-instantaneous plume and often complete a short breath before the ~1.5 s mist has finished, losing the tail of the dose. Scrutinize R6 for `CRIT-RSM-05`. Also expect a reflexive, unnecessary **shaking** of the device (harmless — do not score it as an error) and, conversely, a tendency to skip the base half-turn because pMDI has no loading step (`CRIT-RSM-01`).
- **Either direction**: reassure the patient that coordination demands are lower than with a pMDI — the long emission window is genuinely forgiving — while emphasizing that the base half-turn and vent-free grip are new, device-specific requirements they have never had to perform before.
